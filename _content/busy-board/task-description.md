Busy Board is a <b>tablet-based task</b> in which <hi>children explore cause-and-effect relations</hi> between a variety of different inputs and outputs. It is designed to <b>measure the quantity and quality of self-directed exploration as an index of curiosity</b>. The task also includes a knowledge test after the child ends the exploration period, which is designed to reveal whether exploration resulted in learning of the causal relations between inputs and outputs.

This assessment was inspired by physical “busy board” toys, which are associated with the Montessori curriculum. These toys include a variety of manipulable elements (e.g., buttons, knobs, levers) that children can explore to see what actions they produce. Our computarized Busy Board task presents a colorful display of child-friendly images representing various inputs and outputs on a white background. Inputs are images that change position or look different when activated (e.g., a crank input spins 360 degrees when children interact with it). Each input is linked to at most one output image that changes when the input is activated (e.g., turning the crank causes gears to turn for up to four seconds). Children can choose to end the game at any time if they want to stop playing, and the game ends automatically after six minutes of exploration.
