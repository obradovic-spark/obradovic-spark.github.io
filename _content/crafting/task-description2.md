
Our task is a simplified version of these games because our aim is to assess individual differences in children’s curiosity and motivation to explore in a short period of time. As such, it focuses on the discovery of new objects and does not involve world-building play. Specifically, we provide children with objects that they can combine to discover a new object. These objects are organized into distinct yellow and blue sections of the screen, and children are instructed to combine one yellow and one blue object to make something new. Children begin the game with the ability to combine four basic objects (water, fire, plant, and human) that appear in both yellow and blue spaces of the game. Inclusion of a human icon enables the discovery of thematically relevant human-made objects that are more engaging and developmentally salient to younger children than combinations of just simple materials. By combining pairs of 4 basic objects (including combining each object with itself), children can discover a total of 10 new objects, which then appear in the blue space. By combining 4 basic elements in the yellow space with the 10 new objects in the blue space, children can discover an additional 40 objects (50 total objects possible). Children are told that they can press a button to end the game at any time. The game ends automatically after six minutes of play.
